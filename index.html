Program overview

What the program is, who it is for and the four strategies it rests on
PLE REAP
Medicine

This instructional design sets out how Year 5 medical students of Ateneo de Zamboanga School of Medicine will review all 12 subjects of the Physician Licensure Examination (PLE) while they are deployed in the community. The review runs alongside their deployment: students study and sit short online exams independently in the field, then return to school for a proctored comprehensive examination.

🎓
Program
PLE Review for Year 5 medical students
🤝
Partners
PLE REAP Medicine and Ateneo de Zamboanga School of Medicine
🧑‍⚕️
Learners
Year 5 medical students on community deployment
🗓️
Duration
Conducted within one academic year; the 12 subjects are completed in three 9-week cycles (27 weeks)
🔁
Cycle
8 weeks in the community (4 subjects, 2 weeks each), followed by 1 week at school
📚
Coverage
The 12 PLE subjects, aligned with the Test Blueprint of the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) Board of Medicine
📡
Mode of delivery
Online and self-directed while in the community; onsite, online and proctored during the campus week
📱
Platform
Quick Access (QA) App built by the Consultancy, the single entry point to all exams and resources
📝
Assessment
Per subject: four 50-item online exams (Diagnostic, two Progress Evaluations, Final Sprint) and a 100-item practice set. Per cycle: four 100-item Comprehensive Examinations
🎯
Passing mark
75% in all examinations
💬
Feedback
Rationale for every item, released after each examination
📱 One-year app access. While the review program itself runs for 27 weeks, students keep access to the Quick Access App for one full year.

Design principles

The design rests on four strategies, applied to learners who are away from campus. Tap a card to turn it over.

Delivery. Because students are deployed, teaching is delivered through recorded lecture videos and immersive online resources that students can open at any time.

Learner and needs analysis

Six facts about the learners, and how the design answers each
PLE REAP
Medicine

Six factors shape this design. Choose a factor to see what it means for the learners and how the design responds.

🎓
Factor 1 of 6
Stage of training
What it means for the learners
Year 5 students have finished their basic and clinical sciences and are closest to the licensure examination.
↓
✔ Design response
The program is a review: recall-heavy, integrative and exam-oriented, with new teaching limited to weak topics.
🏘️
Factor 2 of 6
Community deployment
What it means for the learners
Students are off campus for two months at a time and carry community duties during the day.
↓
✔ Design response
Asynchronous resources, a fixed and short daily exam window (1:00–2:00 PM) and a predictable two-week rhythm per subject.
🧑‍💻
Factor 3 of 6
Independent study
What it means for the learners
There is no classroom or lecturer on site, so progress depends on self-directed learning.
↓
✔ Design response
Diagnostic results and rationales guide each student's own study plan; the QA App puts every resource in one place.
📊
Factor 4 of 6
Varied baseline
What it means for the learners
Students differ in which subjects and topics they have retained.
↓
✔ Design response
Diagnostic analytics identify the lowest-performing topics, which each student targets in guided study.
📚
Factor 5 of 6
Breadth of the PLE
What it means for the learners
Twelve subjects must be covered without overloading students who are also in service.
↓
✔ Design response
One subject at a time, in two-week blocks, with resources in several formats (reading, video, flashcards, mnemonics, audio).
⏱️
Factor 6 of 6
Exam readiness
What it means for the learners
Students need practice under timed, supervised conditions before the licensure examination.
↓
✔ Design response
A proctored, full-day Comprehensive Examination at the end of every cycle.

The four subject examinations

ExaminationWhenPurposeHow the result is used
Diagnostic
Examination		Monday, Week 1Measures baseline knowledge of the subject before any review.Analytics identify the lowest-performing topics; the student builds a study plan around them.
Progress
Evaluation 1		Friday, Week 1Checks early gains after the first week of study.Compared with the Diagnostic to confirm which gaps have closed and which remain.
Progress
Evaluation 2		Monday, Week 2Checks retention after the weekend and sets the focus for the second week.Directs the last three study days to the topics still below 75%.
Final SprintFriday, Week 2Summative exam for the subject.Recorded as the subject outcome; remaining weak topics are carried into preparation for the Comprehensive Examination.

Each exam has 50 items, runs for one hour (1:00–2:00 PM), is taken online and independently, and samples the whole subject in proportion to the PRC Board of Medicine Test Blueprint. The four exams use different items, so each one is a fresh retrieval of the same blueprint. The passing mark is 75%, which is 38 of 50 items.

Alignment with the Test Blueprint

  • Every exam is built from the PRC Board of Medicine Test Blueprint for the subject, so the share of items per topic matches the licensure examination.
  • The four 50-item subject exams are parallel forms: the same topic distribution with different items.
  • The 100-item Comprehensive Examination applies the blueprint at full length, matching the 100-item-per-subject format of the review.
  • Results are reported by blueprint topic, so each student can see which topics remain below 75%.

Conduct of examinations

  • Subject exams. Taken independently wherever the student is deployed, within the fixed 1:00–2:00 PM window. Students are expected to work alone and without references.
  • Comprehensive Examination. Taken at school on the students' devices, supervised by proctors.
  • Carry-over exams. A student who fails the Comprehensive Examination of a subject re-takes it in the next cycle until passed; additional days are allotted as necessary.
  • Rationales. Released to all students after the exam window has closed.

Carry-over examinations

A student who scores below 75% in the Comprehensive Examination of a subject re-takes that subject as a carry-over exam in the next cycle, and continues to re-take it until passed. Each subject is treated separately, so a student carries over only the subjects failed.

ElementProvision
WhoStudents below 75% (fewer than 75 of 100 items) in the Comprehensive Examination of a subject
WhatA 100-item Comprehensive Examination in the failed subject, proctored, with a passing mark of 75%
WhenIn the next cycle, alongside that cycle's four subjects
Additional daysAllotted for carry-over subjects as necessary, beyond the regular examination day
Until whenRepeated in each succeeding cycle until the student passes
